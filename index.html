A little curiosity

Small particles. Big questions.

Particle physics explores what the world is made of and how its smallest ingredients behave.

01

What are we made of?

Air, water and our bodies are made of atoms. Atoms contain electrons and a nucleus; inside the nucleus are protons and neutrons, which contain quarks. As far as experiments can tell, electrons and quarks have no smaller parts.

CERN · Inside matter ↗
02

How do we find the invisible?

Particles leave clues as they pass through a detector: tiny electrical signals or deposits of energy. We piece these clues together to work out what happened in a collision—even when a particle decayed before reaching the detector.

CMS at CERN · Following the clues ↗
03

Where did the antimatter go?

The electron has an antimatter partner, the positron: the same mass, but opposite charge. Yet the observable Universe is overwhelmingly made of matter. Why? Comparing particles with their antiparticles helps us look for clues to this still-open question.
